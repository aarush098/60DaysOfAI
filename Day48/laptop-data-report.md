**Conflict — Price:** AppleInsider's price tracker lists the 13" line starting at **$999** (https://prices.appleinsider.com/product/macbook-air-13-inch-m3/MXCV3LL/A), while Laptop Mag and Tom's Guide both independently report **$1,099** as the base MSRP.

*Resolution:* Used Apple's official 18-hour figure (video playback) for consistency with how other vendors' battery claims are sourced (manufacturer-rated), and noted in the tool that lab-tested, real-world numbers typically run lower.

| Performance | 7/10 (analyst-interpreted) | TechRadar review verdict | https://www.techradar.com/computing/windows-laptops/dell-xps-13-intel-core-ultra-2nd-gen-2025-review |
| RAM / Storage | 16GB / 512GB | Laptop Mag | https://www.laptopmag.com/laptops/windows-laptops/dell-xps-13-9350 |

**Conflict — Which XPS 13 model:** Three different "Dell XPS 13" variants surfaced in research: the Snapdragon X Elite 9345 (Live Science reports 27h39m battery), the Intel Lunar Lake 9350 (TechRadar reports ~24h), and the older 2024 Snapdragon 9340 (Tom's Guide, starting $1,299).
*Resolution:* Standardized on the **9350 (Intel Core Ultra 7 258V, Lunar Lake)** throughout, since it's Dell's current mainstream 2024 configuration and the one Laptop Mag priced and speced in detail. Figures from the Snapdragon variants were excluded to avoid mixing specs across different chip platforms.

**Conflict — Battery test method:** Notebookcheck/Tom's Hardware (per aggregator frontdeskreview.com) measured 17.5–20+ hours on the LCD panel using their own lab protocol, versus TechRadar's ~24 hours on video playback.
*Resolution:* Used TechRadar's figure as the primary source since it's a first-party, directly-read review; the lower Notebookcheck/Tom's Hardware range is noted in the tool as a methodology caveat (different test = different result, not a factual contradiction).

**Conflict — Price:** Reported prices for the X1 Carbon Gen 12 ranged wildly: StorageReview's base config starts at **$1,499**; Tom's Hardware's tested unit (16GB/512GB, no OLED) starts at **$2,263**; Laptop Mag's OLED review unit was **$2,199**; Notebookcheck reported a discounted sale price of **$1,099** (down from a $2,099 list).
*Resolution:* Used StorageReview's **$1,499** base-config price as the representative figure, since a budget-conscious student is most likely to buy the entry trim rather than the OLED/high-RAM review units other outlets tested. **Conflict — Battery life:** StorageReview measured 14h40m (PCMark10), while ITPro's Core Ultra 5 test unit measured only **10h29m**, and Tom's Hardware called battery life "subpar" without giving a precise figure (OLED panel).
*Resolution:* Used StorageReview's 14h40m as it's the most precisely documented, methodology-labeled figure. The wide spread (10h29m–14h40m depending on panel type and workload) is noted as a real methodology/config difference, not an error — OLED and IPS panel variants of this laptop have meaningfully different battery life.

*Resolution:* Used the 14h30m video-looping figure as the representative "everyday use" number (most comparable to how other laptops' battery figures were sourced), while flagging in the tool that workload type drastically changes this laptop's real-world battery life — this is a genuine methodology-dependent result, not a data error.

## Notes on the Performance criterion generally
No single benchmark (e.g., Geekbench) was available across all four laptops from directly-read sources, and the four machines span three chip architectures (Apple Silicon, Intel Core Ultra, Qualcomm Snapdragon variants in related models). Rather than mixing incompatible benchmark numbers, Performance scores (1–10) were derived by the analyst from each outlet's own verdict language and are explicitly flagged in the comparison tool as **interpreted, not raw sourced data**.

## Notes on Storage/RAM value
This criterion is a calculated metric — (RAM in GB + Storage in GB ÷ 10) ÷ price — built from the sourced RAM, storage, and price figures above. It is not itself a number published by any reviewer.
